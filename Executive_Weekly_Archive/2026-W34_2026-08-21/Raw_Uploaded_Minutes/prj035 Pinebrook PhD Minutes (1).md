### **1\. Meeting Objective / Purpose**

To review site sections, address discrepancies in level differences between engineering and landscape models, align on retaining wall strategies, and evaluate the impacts of council density requirements on the urban design framework for Parcel D.

### **2\. Executive Summary**

The team met to coordinate civil engineering plans with landscape designs for Pinebrook Parcel D. Key discussions focused on retaining wall aesthetics versus structural necessity, concluding that a two-tier retaining strategy using brick walls with concrete fill is preferred over gabions in space-constrained areas. The team addressed site layout issues, including shifting house rows to meet the 5m garage setback requirement and locating the mini-substation near the roadside. Regulatory concerns regarding the potential H3 occupancy classification (which demands strict gradient requirements) were discussed; the project will proceed as a single parcel to avoid subdivision delays. The council's current dissatisfaction with the project's density was noted, necessitating a resubmission of the urban design framework.

### **3\. Agenda Items & Discussion Notes**

* **Site and Structural Review:**  
  * Discussed discrepancies between civil and landscape models. Agreed to prioritize engineering structural viability, then apply landscape aesthetics.  
  * Explored "scallop" wall designs; while aesthetically pleasing, their feasibility depends on specific site levels.  
  * Evaluated retaining options: A 2x1 gabion approach requires additional protective walls. Opted for a two-tier retaining strategy to manage costs and limit visual impact.  
  * Determined that brick walls are favored over gabions for property boundaries due to space constraints and long-term stability concerns (gabions may settle and become unstable over time).  
* **Substation Location:**  
  * Evaluated "Location A" vs. "Location B" for the mini-substation.  
  * Agreed to a roadside location to balance cost savings on internal installations while keeping the unit out of the primary public domain. It will be screened with low walls and hedging.  
* **Roadway and Setbacks:**  
  * Identified a critical issue where the garage setback is currently 3m instead of the required 5m.  
  * Decided to shift specific rows of houses back by approximately 1 meter to create necessary parking and turning space, mitigating the risk of devaluing the properties.  
* **Green Space and Pathways:**  
  * Acknowledged steep slopes (1:3 and 1:2) in green belts.  
  * Agreed to implement a fine exposed aggregate (sandstone color) for pathways.  
  * Decided against continuous roadside walkways if internal networks are sufficient.  
* **Drainage and Stormwater:**  
  * Confirmed the road will be sloped toward the higher side of properties to intercept stormwater, preventing runoff into lower-lying units.  
  * Sewer and stormwater lines will be shifted toward the center of the site.  
* **Regulatory and Density Challenges:**  
  * **Occupancy Risk:** The site risks classification as H3 (two or more dwellings on a single site) instead of H4, which triggers strict 1:25 gradient rules for the first 5 meters.  
  * Subdividing the site to avoid H3 was rejected due to timeline and fee implications.  
  * **Council Impasse:** The council wants higher density/low-cost housing. The team must resubmit the urban design framework to address this.

## **Decisions Made**

* **Decision 1**: The perimeter boundary will utilize a low wall (200-400mm) with clear view fencing, while using gabions for retaining along the remainder of the boundary.  
* **Decision 2**: Implement a two-tier retaining wall design rather than a single 2m high retaining wall to optimize costs and site requirements.  
* **Decision 3**: The mini substation location is set to the roadside position, acting as a compromise to keep it out of the public domain while maintaining accessibility.  
* **Decision 4**: The development layout will be adjusted by shifting specific units/rows back to ensure the required 5m distance from the garage to the road is achieved, allowing for parking.  
* **Decision 5**: The chosen pathway finish is a fine exposed aggregate in a sandstone color.  
* **Decision 6**: The requirement for a continuous roadside walkway is removed, relying instead on the existing network of internal walkways.  
* **Decision 7**: Gabions will be excluded from green space areas where they are not appropriate or feasible; brick walls with concrete fill are selected as the preferred material for boundary conditions.  
* **Decision 8**: The road will be sloped toward the higher side of properties to intercept stormwater and prevent runoff into lower-lying plots.  
* **Decision 9**: The property will remain a single site rather than being subdivided, managing the associated design challenges.  
* **Decision 10**: Terraforce is selected as the cost-effective retaining method for the verge areas near driveways.
