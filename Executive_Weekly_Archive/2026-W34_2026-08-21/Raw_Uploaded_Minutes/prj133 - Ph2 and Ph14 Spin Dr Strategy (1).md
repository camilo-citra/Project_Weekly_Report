### **1\. Meeting Objective / Purpose**

To formally document and escalate severe structural engineering concerns observed in the Phase 14 houses at CentralBlue, specifically relating to cracking, water ingress, and structural stability of the Citra panels.

### **2\. Executive Summary**

Jacques Kruger, Head of Engineering at Citra SA, presented a formal letter detailing significant structural issues observed post-construction on the CentralBlue Phase 14 project. The concerns include extensive cracking, severe water leaks, and a visibly leaning wall. Despite previous communications and site visits with the appointed external engineer (iX Engineers), who deemed the issues aesthetic or related to workmanship, the internal Citra engineering team strongly believes these are fundamental structural failures requiring immediate, professional re-evaluation. Additionally, new material durability concerns regarding the MgO board have been raised.

### **3\. Agenda Items & Discussion Notes**

* **Initial Observations:**  
  * Extensive internal and external cracking on Citra walls and roofs, worsening over a 6-month period, located at high-stress areas.  
  * Extreme water leaks through panels, causing puddling, indicating water retention within the panels.  
  * Excessive outward leaning of a Citra wall on the second storey, adjacent to an expansion joint lacking lateral support.  
* **Previous Engineer Engagements (iX Engineers):**  
  * First site visit (March 12, 2025): Engineer deemed cracks aesthetic. Repairs failed (cracks returned).  
  * Second site visit (April 8, 2025): Engineer maintained cracks were non-structural.  
  * Engineer's Report (May 7, 2025): Remodelled building accounting for the expansion joint (lacking shear transfer). Concluded design was structurally sound based on uncracked capacities provided by Citra SA. Blamed construction tolerances, workmanship, lack of control joints, and differential movement.  
* **Citra Engineering Assessment:**  
  * Cracks are structural (\>2mm) and exceed serviceability limits.  
  * The 25mm plaster layer carries the structural load; full-thickness cracks are critical.  
  * Failed repairs indicate an underlying structural problem.  
  * Engineer's analysis used uncracked capacities, inflating safety.  
  * A safety factor of 1.5 is insufficient for novel materials; a higher factor (2.3+) is required.  
  * Lack of shear transfer at the expansion joint is causing a 5-10mm deflection (stability issue).  
  * Omission of control joints in early units exacerbated cracking.  
  * Use of basalt fibre reinforcement (less stiff, brittle failure) worsens cracking compared to steel.  
  * Concerns regarding site inspection frequency and quality management by iX Engineers.  
* **Material Durability Discoveries (R\&D):**  
  * MgO board has 15% water absorption, reducing bending and shear strength by 30% after 3 wet/dry cycles.  
  * MgO board strength varies by 25% (32% when wet) depending on orientation due to internal mesh.  
  * High chloride content (2.2%) in MgO boards poses a severe corrosion risk to reinforcement and fixings.

## **Decisions Made**

* **Decision 1**: Escalate the structural concerns officially to Cheryl Hillman (Citra Development).  
* **Decision 2**: Recommend an independent design review and structural assessment by a professional engineer, in line with ECSA prescriptions for complex engineering activities.

## **Issue Register**

* Issue: Severe water ingress through cracks leading to internal flooding and panel saturation. **Root Cause**: Structural cracking compromising the waterproof envelope combined with the 15% water absorption rate of the MgO boards. **Proposed Solution**: Develop a comprehensive waterproofing and crack repair strategy pending the independent structural review.  
* Issue: Citra wall on second storey leaning outward by 5-10mm. **Root Cause**: Lack of shear transfer mechanism (dowels) at the expansion joint, leading to a lack of lateral stability. **Proposed Solution**: Independent structural engineer to design and implement an immediate structural remediation plan for the affected units.
